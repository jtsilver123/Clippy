Clippy Privacy Policy

Last updated: April 8, 2026

Clippy is a Chrome extension that lets you talk to Claude about the web page you're looking at. This page explains exactly what data Clippy touches, where it goes, and what it never does.

The short version

  • Clippy has no backend. There is no Clippy server.
  • Your Anthropic API key, license key, and settings live in your browser's local storage. They never leave your computer except as described below.
  • Your voice transcript and a screenshot of the current tab are sent directly from your browser to Anthropic so Claude can answer. We never see them.
  • Your license key is sent directly from your browser to Gumroad for verification. We never see it.
  • Clippy does not collect analytics, telemetry, advertising IDs, cookies, or any personal information.

What Clippy stores on your computer

Clippy uses Chrome's chrome.storage.local API to save the following on your local machine:

  • Your selected AI provider (Anthropic or Google Gemini)
  • Your Anthropic API key, if you saved one
  • Your Google Gemini API key, if you saved one
  • Your Gumroad license key
  • Your selected model for each provider
  • Whether your license has been verified

You can delete all of this at any time by clicking Reset Clippy in the extension popup, or by uninstalling the extension.

What Clippy sends over the network

Clippy makes network requests to at most three domains, and only three. Which two of the three depends on which AI provider you picked in the popup:

  • api.anthropic.com — only used if you picked "Anthropic Claude" as your AI provider. When you press the push-to-talk shortcut, Clippy sends your spoken transcript, a PNG screenshot of the visible portion of your current tab, and your conversation history on the current page directly to Anthropic's Messages API. Authenticated with your own Anthropic API key. Anthropic's privacy policy applies to that traffic. See anthropic.com/legal/privacy.
  • generativelanguage.googleapis.com — only used if you picked "Google Gemini" as your AI provider. Clippy sends the same data (spoken transcript, screenshot, conversation history) directly to Google's Gemini API, authenticated with your own Google AI Studio API key. Google's privacy policy applies to that traffic. See policies.google.com/privacy.
  • api.gumroad.com — when you click Activate in the popup, Clippy sends your license key to Gumroad's public license verification endpoint to confirm the key was actually purchased. Gumroad's privacy policy applies to that traffic. See gumroad.com/privacy.

What Clippy never does

  • Clippy never sends data to a server we operate. We don't have one.
  • Clippy never reads, stores, or transmits the contents of pages you visit unless you actively press the push-to-talk shortcut. At that moment it captures a single PNG of the visible viewport and sends it to Anthropic. It does not capture pages in the background.
  • Clippy never enables the microphone unless you press the push-to-talk shortcut. Speech recognition runs locally in Chrome via the built-in Web Speech API.
  • Clippy does not use tracking cookies, fingerprinting, ad identifiers, analytics SDKs, or third-party trackers.
  • Clippy does not sell, share, or rent any data, because it never collects any.

Permissions Clippy requests, and why

  • activeTab, tabs, scripting: needed to capture a screenshot of the current tab and draw the Clippy overlay on it when you press the shortcut.
  • storage: needed to remember your API key, license key, and settings between browser restarts.
  • Host permission for api.anthropic.com: needed to send your transcript and screenshot to Claude.
  • Host permission for api.gumroad.com: needed to verify your license key.

Children

Clippy is not directed at children under 13. Clippy doesn't collect personal information from anyone, so by extension it doesn't collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If Clippy ever starts doing anything different from what's described here, we'll update this page and bump the "last updated" date. The current version is always at the URL where you're reading this.
